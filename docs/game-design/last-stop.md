# Last Stop — design blueprint

Short (5–6 hours) first-person psychological / survival horror game. **This file is the source of truth**: read it before planning any feature, and add every new creative decision here.

## Premise

- **Player:** a night-shift maintenance worker. After a city-wide blackout they are sent down alone to bring power back to the metro.
- **Setting:** a 1990s US-style city (NYC-like tiled stations, "Uptown & The Bronx"-style signs).
- **Route:** an eerie street → stairs down into the metro → a "Staff Only" door → maintenance tunnels.
- **Goal:** replace fuses in fuse boxes and fix generators to restore power to the area's stations.

## Structure

- **4 stations** (about 1–1.5 hours each), linked by tunnels, then a final ending zone.
- Each station is a zone whose power the player restores.

- **Rare other horrors** appear only in scripted moments.

The code stays; the locker and cabinet are simply empty of them. The creative director wants more useful consumables and collectibles instead.
  - **Consumables (2026-10-23):** **road flares** replace the shotgun as the only "defence": G strikes one and holds it low in the right hand (bright, flickering red light and a fizz for 30 s, fading over the last 4 s); G again drops it, still burning, a little ahead of you. Later they will keep the stalker back. Two in this area: on the staff locker shelf and by the wall deep in the west tunnel. No flare model yet (a red stick made of basic shapes). **Painkillers**: two bottles (staff desk, blue platform cabinet), collected and counted only until the player has health (planned with the stalker).
  - **Inventory screen (2026-10-23):** Tab opens it (Tab or Esc closes it); the game pauses and goes quiet. Drawn on the FREE PSX UI frame (skin 1, dark wine). Left: the selected item's model turning slowly (a small chunky render), with its name below. Right: what you carry with counts (W/S, arrows, mouse wheel or click to select), and a short description. Bottom left (covering the frame's printed menu words): torch battery bar, fuse box progress, power on/off.
  - **Menus and settings (2026-10-23):** the game opens on a **title screen**: a slowly swaying view down the east tunnel at the stalled train's flickering cab light, "LAST STOP / LINE 4 - HOLLIS ST", press any key, then New game / Options / Credits / Quit (New game fades in to the street). **Esc** pauses (game frozen and silent): Resume / Options / Credits / Quit to title / Quit game (quits ask YES/NO, NO first). **Options:** master, music and effects-&-ambience volume; mouse sensitivity and invert Y; brightness, PS1 filter (0-30%), field of view (75-110, default 90), viewmodel FOV (45-75, default 60: held items are drawn in their own pass); item glow; centre dot; fullscreen and vsync (built game only); reset to defaults. Style: the FREE PSX UI look (wine panels, ">>" lists, ticked sliders, [ON / OFF]) with the **VCR OSD Mono** font, also used by the HUD and inventory.
  - **Key bindings and controller (2026-10-23):** Options / Controls / **Key bindings** lists every action (move, sprint, crouch, jump, lean, use, flashlight, swap battery, flare, inventory, pause); choose one and press the new key or mouse button (Esc cancels; a key already in use swaps places); Reset keys restores them. **Controller** (fixed layout, shown under Controller layout): left stick move, right stick look, L3 sprint (stays on until you stop), A jump, B crouch, X use, Y flare, LB/RB lean, d-pad up flashlight, d-pad down battery, View inventory, Menu pause; menus use d-pad/stick, A, B.   - **Frankie replaces the Pale Man (2026-10-24):** the monster is now **Frankie** (the Big Frankie model: a gaunt, long-armed creature with bulging eyes and bared teeth), **2.1 m** tall, his **top hat removed**. He uses **his own animations**: a hunched walk, a run, a "scary idle", a roar (kept as his scream) and an attack cut from his door animation (a crouched lunge with a claw reach; the hit lands 0.8 s in). Everything below written for the Pale Man (senses, moods, speeds, flares, lockers, staged first sighting, torch flicker, sounds, the train finale) now applies to Frankie unchanged. Animation playback is matched to his measured foot speeds (walk 0.8 m/s, run about 5.5 m/s, so his run plays a little slower than authored at 3.8 m/s).
  - **The Pale Man (2026-10-23), the stalker (now Frankie):** a tall, thin, pale figure with a wide black grin (Character_Monster from Characters PSX 1.2; it replaces TheWrapper as the monster, whose animations he uses: nervous walk, frantic run, watchful idle, scream, lunge). He is nowhere until the fuse box is fixed (since 2026-10-27: until you first reach the bottom of the service stairs; see "Hollis St, Frankie wakes earlier"); **5-10 s after the power returns** (was 15-20 s until 2026-10-24) every lamp stutters, a shriek echoes from deep in the west tunnel and he starts hunting from there. **Senses:** sees you within ~15 m (25 m if your torch is on), in front of him, if nothing blocks the view; hears running within 15 m, walking within 6 m, crouching not at all, and a struck flare within 8 m. **Moods:** wanders at 0.9 m/s (slowed from 1.4 on 2026-10-24 so his feet stay planted; sometimes drifting toward you), investigates noises, chases (3.8 m/s; your sprint is 4.3), searches where he lost you (gives up after ~8 s without sight), lunges when within ~1.6 m, then backs off for 6 s so hits never come back to back. **Flares:** he keeps about 6 m from any lit flare (held or dropped) and backs away from it. **Health:** two hits kill you (red flash and a jolt; while hurt the screen edges stay red and you breathe hard); painkillers from the inventory heal one hit; death fades to black ("YOU DIED") and returns to the title (load a save from there). He walks only underground (the street is safe) and cannot reach the staff room (its door is shut when his walkable area is made). Chase music (Svirls, "Chase 3" since 2026-10-29; was "Chase 1") plays only during chases. His sounds are reshaped from the Darkworld pack with ffmpeg (a wet rattling breath, a reversed in-breath sucking into a two-voice shriek, bare fleshy footsteps, joint cracks every 5-12 s while he moves) until real monster sounds are found.
  - **Pale Man polish (2026-10-24):** **First sighting (staged):** after the arrival shriek the lamps stutter again and he appears under the lamp at the platform end further from the player, staring; when the player first sees him a sting plays (Svirls, "Jumpscare"), he stares 2.5 s more, then walks into that end's tunnel without chasing, and only then starts hunting (he also leaves if the player comes within 5 m or after 30 s). **Hiding lockers:** four (a pair on the platform's back wall, one in each tunnel); use one to step in and peek through the door slats (you can turn about 55 degrees each way); he can't see you inside unless he saw you get in, in which case he walks over, yanks the door open and attacks. **Torch flicker:** within 10 m and out of view, your torch stutters, more often the closer he is. Difficulty kept as tested. **Animation (2026-10-24):** his feet no longer skate: the walk and run animations play faster to match his real speed (measured: the walk's feet move 0.28 m/s, the run's 1.07 m/s; playback capped at 3.25x walking, 3x running), walk/run switching is smoothed, he turns more slowly (180 degrees/s), and footstep sounds land on the animation's footfalls.
  - **Frankie's reach (2026-10-26):** he lunges when you're within **2 m** (was 1.6 m) and the hit lands if you're still within **2.5 m** (was 2.1 m) when his claws come down, so he's harder to juke.
  - **Frankie, more dangerous (2026-10-26):** **sight depends on light.** In pitch black with the torch off he sees you only within **4 m**; by a candle or a red emergency lamp about 6 m; standing under working lamps **15 m**; with your torch on **25 m**. **He no longer gives up after one hit:** after each swing he takes a **3-second breather** (a step back, staring at you), then comes straight back at you, until you die, escape out of sight, hide unseen or drive him off with a flare.
  - **Playtest fixes (2026-10-27)** (first friend playtest: "the AI is too easy, he never hit me"):
    - **The swipe lunges:** when you're within 2 m he leaps about **1.5 m forward** and the claws land after **0.5 s** (was 0.8 s standing still; the animation is sped up to match). Once he starts he turns only slowly, and the claws only catch you **in front of him** (within 50 degrees). Walking or running straight away gets you hit; a **sideways dodge** early in the swipe makes him miss; dodging too late doesn't.
    - **A miss means no breather:** he comes straight back at you. The 3 s breather only follows a hit that landed.
    - **Sprint stamina:** sprinting lasts about **6 s**, then you're **winded** and can only jog at **3.0 m/s** (Frankie runs 3.8) until you've got back 40% (about 3 s of not sprinting; full again after 8 s). No bar on screen: the heavy breathing tells you, building as stamina drains.
    - **He jumps anywhere along the platform edges:** jump links every 4 m along every platform edge at both stations (was 3-4 narrow spots, so he often walked the long way round to the tracks), and he **leaps in a short arc** (about 0.8 s) instead of gliding.
    - "I should turn around..." no longer appears on the Canal St escalators (it only belongs near the street's invisible walls).
- **Flashlight:** the battery drains and spare batteries are scarce.
- **Saving:** only in **safe rooms** (e.g. a staff break room with a working lamp).
  - **Built (2026-10-24):** the staff room PC is the save point ("Use computer (save game)"), plus **one autosave** 4 s after the power comes back (its own slot, so it never overwrites the player's). **5 named slots.** Choosing a slot opens a name box pre-filled with "<area> - <time>" (e.g. "Staff Room - 14:32"); Enter keeps it or type your own (24 letters max). **Load game** is on the title and in the pause menu (from the pause menu it asks first). A save keeps: where you stand, what you carry, torch battery and on/off, hits taken, fuses in the box, power, picked-up items, open doors, and whether the Pale Man is awake and where.
- **No sanity mechanic.**

## Look

- **Surface brightness:** concrete and gravel tints stay around 0.5–0.6. Near-black surfaces made the flashlight look useless in the tunnels.

- **Cleaner PS1:** low-poly PSX models with crunchy pixel-sharp textures, at **full resolution**, with no vertex wobble and no pixelation filter.
- **Lighting:** pools of light with near-black darkness between them.
  - Stations: mostly cold, greenish fluorescent tubes.
  - Street: warm sodium-orange lamps.
  - Tunnels: red emergency lights.
- **Fog:** things fade into black over about **25 m** (raised from 15 m after playtesting), but bright lamps still glow through from further away.

## Sound
- **Sound pass (2026-10-22):** each area has its own background sound, crossfading as you walk (`AreaAmbience`): street = rain loop + quiet "Tube Wind" drone (the rain muffles down the stairs and dies out along the corridor); station = "Too Quiet"; tunnels = "Always Observing". Underground, a distant noise (rockfall, gravel, metal scrape, door creak) plays every 60-120 s from 15-30 m away (`DistantNoises`). A leaking pipe loops above the track puddle. Footsteps: wet gravel on the tracks, splashes in puddles, concrete elsewhere. Notes and posters rustle when you start reading them; fuses clink, the key rattles.

- **Ambience, tension and machine hums:** Helton Yan's Surreal Drones (53-second loops).
- **One-shot effects:** Darkworld Audio – Survival Effects (footsteps, doors, switches, water, breathing, gun handling).
- **Music:** "PSX Horror Music" by Svirls, used **rarely**: chases, big reveals, power coming back, and credits.
- **Shotgun blast:** silent until a real shotgun sound is found.
- **Character sounds (2026-10-26):** Frankie's **scream when he spots you** (start of every chase) is now a real monster scream: the strongest 3 s of one of two close screams, with a quick fade. His **far-away echoing scream** plays when he wakes deep in Hollis St's west tunnel, and at Canal St from far back down the arrival tunnel about **6 s before** the thud on the train roof. The **player breathes**: heavy breathing builds up as sprint stamina drains (since 2026-10-27; it was a separate 4 s timer) and dies away as it refills; in a **locker** a slow, held breath plays, louder the closer Frankie is (from 10 m in). His breaths, footsteps and joint cracks are still the reshaped Darkworld sounds.
- **Still missing:** a shotgun blast, footsteps on metal grates, and real breathing/footstep sounds for Frankie.

## Station 2: Canal St Junction (built 2026-10-25)

- **Look:** a busy interchange gone dead: an 80 m white-tiled hall with a blue band, an island platform between two tracks, columns, benches; escalators up to a mezzanine with ticket gates, a ticket booth and ticket machines.
- **Arrival:** when the Hollis St train pulls away the screen fades to black and the ride rumbles on; you wake in a car that **stalls short** in the tunnel, 40 m before the station. Its light stutters and dies; you walk the rest along the track toward a red emergency lamp. The game autosaves ("Autosave - Canal St Junction").
- **Frankie follows you:** about **60 s** after you arrive a heavy thud sounds on the stalled train's roof; he drops onto the tracks and hunts as usual.
- **Goal: restart the backup generator** (service room off the mezzanine). It needs **2 fuel cans** (since 2026-10-27: one in the station master's office, one in the stores cage on the lower level; they used to be on the platform and at the end of the south tunnel) and **a spark plug**, locked in the **station master's office**; the office key lies on the ticket booth's back counter. The first pull of the starter cord only coughs (Frankie may hear it), the second roars into life (loud enough for the whole station). Canal St has its **own power**: lights come on in a wave, the escalators start, and the **street shutter** at the top rolls up.
- **Ending:** (since 2026-10-27) climb down the **emergency latch** on the lower level, released by the generator; it shows **"END OF STATION 2 / CANAL ST JUNCTION"**. (Until then the way out was the street gate at the top of the escalators, which is now jammed.)

## Station 3: the sewers (planned 2026-10-28, NOT built yet)

- **Fear: drowning / water.** Reached by climbing down Canal St's **emergency latch** into the overflow tunnels under Line 4.
- **Size:** like Canal St, about 1-1.5 hours: a **pumping station hub** plus **three branches**.
- **Goal: drain the flood.** The sewers are flooding. **Open three valves, one in each branch.** Each valve lowers the water a little, which opens the way into the next branch. Then **restart the main pump** in the hub.
- **Water in play: it rises and falls.** Some areas are knee- or waist-deep: wading is slow and loud. Deep water can't be walked through, only crossed on ledges and gantries. Each valve, and finally the pump, lowers the water and opens new paths.
- **The danger is the water itself (no creature for now):** deep water drowns you if you stay under too long, with the breath shown **by sound only** (no meter on screen). Strong currents in some channels push you, and falling in is the main threat. **Frankie does not follow** into the sewers. A **new, water-based monster** will be added later by the director.
- **Exit: the river outfall.** Once the pump runs, the overflow tunnel drains and leads out to the river, as the latch notice says. Station 4 (losing your mind) starts back underground somewhere else.
- **Voicemails (2):** an old wall phone in the **pump control room** (visible), and an emergency phone **deep in a flooded branch** (hidden, reachable only after draining it). Who speaks is still to decide (his father stays for the very end).
- **Assets to use:** the Sewer pack's textures already in the project (brick, dirty concrete, rusty metal, mud), the industrial kit from the lower levels (pipes, pumps, control boxes, chain-link, caged bulbs), and the water and splash sounds.
- **First draft built (2026-10-28):** climbing down Canal St's emergency latch now fades out and leads into the sewers (replacing the "End of Station 2" card; it autosaves "Autosave - The Sewers"; Frankie stays behind). A walkable greybox in sewer brick, about 20 m below and beyond z 600:
  - **arrival chamber** with a ladder up to the shut hatch;
  - **main tunnel**: a walkway beside a knee-deep channel;
  - **pumping-station hub** (16 x 16 m, 6 m high): a central sump of water, the main pump (placeholder drum and motor), a control room in the north-west corner;
  - **west branch** (knee-deep), **east branch** (a step down, waist-deep), **north branch** (deep, crossed on a narrow ledge along the west wall), each ending in a **valve room** with a red valve wheel (placeholder);

## The story: Sousa, the failing line, and fear (revised 2026-10-28)

- **The giant spider reveal is dropped**, and so is "fear made flesh" as a confirmed truth: **no one is confirmed to have been turned into a monster.**
- **The real reason Line 4 is dead is mundane: years of cost-cutting.** Postponed repairs, old equipment, budgets cut year after year, until tonight it all failed at once. **The accidents feel real**: the west train crushed by a tunnel collapse, dead power feeds, flooding and fires further down the line are failures of neglect, not curses. The newspaper, the van radio and notices hint at it ("held together with tape").
- **The missing workers (Dale Kowalski, Rosa Mendez, Theo Banks) are removed from the story**, with their posters, the newspaper story, Rosa's payphone message and the wrapped shape on the stairs (removed from the game 2026-10-28).
- **The haunting is never explained.** Frankie, the shadow figures and the creatures of each section could be real, or Sousa's mind under stress and derealisation. The game gives evidence both ways and never decides.
- **The focus is Sousa.** The creatures embody **his** fears, one per section; nothing says where they come from.
- **Creatures (assets):**
  - **Frankie now has the Ridgeback's body** (Codyanka, built 2026-10-28; the AI, name and sounds are unchanged). The Wrapped and Clawman (Codyanka) remain options for later creatures.
  - **Necrothread: The Pulse of Rotting Flesh** (imaginais) is for **later**, as its own monster; the rest of imaginais' monsters (especially the **eyeball spider creatures**) fit too.
- **Shadow figures** (built 2026-10-28) at the corner of your eye or the edge of the darkness: there for a moment, gone when you look. Never explained.
- **Structure:** **each section of the game focuses on one fear**, and the **last one is the player character's own fear**.
- **The player character (decided 2026-10-28):** a **Portuguese migrant worker**, estranged from his family and friends because of deep-seated mental struggles (ADHD, anger issues, depression, derealisation). He deeply regrets his own existence and how he handled things in the past with his **parents** and his **former girlfriend**. No kids: just regrets and a bitter life. That's why he takes the job: nothing left to lose, nothing worth losing, or so he thinks. He can still lose his mind.
  - **His fear: being forgotten.** The final section is built from it.
  - **Revealed by slow hints** scattered through every station, small personal things that only make sense at the end.
  - **Two endings** (see below).
  - **Name: J. Sousa.** Workmates, dispatch and badges use only his surname ("Sousa", "J. Sousa").

  - **He rarely speaks**: a few short muttered lines at key moments, some in Portuguese, shown as subtitles.
  - **The core memory the game builds toward: the fight with his father**, the night it all broke, which he has never forgiven himself for. The hopeful ending is making peace with it.
