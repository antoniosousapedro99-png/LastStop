# Last Stop — design blueprint

Short (5–6 hours) first-person psychological / survival horror game. **This file is the source of truth**: read it before planning any feature, and add every new creative decision here.

## Premise

- **Player:** a night-shift maintenance worker. After a city-wide blackout they are sent down alone to bring power back to the metro.
- **Setting:** a 1990s US-style city (NYC-like tiled stations, "Uptown & The Bronx"-style signs).
- **Route:** an eerie street → stairs down into the metro → a "Staff Only" door → maintenance tunnels.
- **Goal:** replace fuses in fuse boxes and fix generators to restore power to the area's stations.

## Structure

- **4 stations** (about 1–1.5 hours each), linked by tunnels, then a final ending zone.
- Each station is a zone whose power the player restores.

He uses **his own animations**: a hunched walk, a run, a "scary idle", a roar (kept as his scream) and an attack cut from his door animation (a crouched lunge with a claw reach; the hit lands 0.8 s in). Animation playback is matched to his measured foot speeds (walk 0.8 m/s, run about 5.5 m/s, so his run plays a little slower than authored at 3.8 m/s).
  - **The Pale Man (2026-10-23), the stalker (now Frankie):** a tall, thin, pale figure with a wide black grin (Character_Monster from Characters PSX 1.2; it replaces TheWrapper as the monster, whose animations he uses: nervous walk, frantic run, watchful idle, scream, lunge). He is nowhere until the fuse box is fixed (since 2026-10-27: until you first reach the bottom of the service stairs; see "Hollis St, Frankie wakes earlier"); **5-10 s after the power returns** (was 15-20 s until 2026-10-24) every lamp stutters, a shriek echoes from deep in the west tunnel and he starts hunting from there. **Senses:** sees you within ~15 m (25 m if your torch is on), in front of him, if nothing blocks the view; hears running within 15 m, walking within 6 m, crouching not at all, and a struck flare within 8 m. **Moods:** wanders at 0.9 m/s (slowed from 1.4 on 2026-10-24 so his feet stay planted; sometimes drifting toward you), investigates noises, chases (3.8 m/s; your sprint is 4.3), searches where he lost you (gives up after ~8 s without sight), lunges when within ~1.6 m, then backs off for 6 s so hits never come back to back. **Flares:** he keeps about 6 m from any lit flare (held or dropped) and backs away from it. **Health:** two hits kill you (red flash and a jolt; while hurt the screen edges stay red and you breathe hard); painkillers from the inventory heal one hit; death fades to black ("YOU DIED") and returns to the title (load a save from there). He walks only underground (the street is safe) and cannot reach the staff room (its door is shut when his walkable area is made). Chase music (Svirls, "Chase 1") plays only during chases. His sounds are reshaped from the Darkworld pack with ffmpeg (a wet rattling breath, a reversed in-breath sucking into a two-voice shriek, bare fleshy footsteps, joint cracks every 5-12 s while he moves) until real monster sounds are found.
  - **Pale Man polish (2026-10-24):** **First sighting (staged):** after the arrival shriek the lamps stutter again and he appears under the lamp at the platform end further from the player, staring; when the player first sees him a sting plays (Svirls, "Jumpscare"), he stares 2.5 s more, then walks into that end's tunnel without chasing, and only then starts hunting (he also leaves if the player comes within 5 m or after 30 s). **Hiding lockers:** four (a pair on the platform's back wall, one in each tunnel); use one to step in and peek through the door slats (you can turn about 55 degrees each way); he can't see you inside unless he saw you get in, in which case he walks over, yanks the door open and attacks. **Torch flicker:** within 10 m and out of view, your torch stutters, more often the closer he is. Difficulty kept as tested. **Animation (2026-10-24):** his feet no longer skate: the walk and run animations play faster to match his real speed (measured: the walk's feet move 0.28 m/s, the run's 1.07 m/s; playback capped at 3.25x walking, 3x running), walk/run switching is smoothed, he turns more slowly (180 degrees/s), and footstep sounds land on the animation's footfalls.
  - **Frankie's reach (2026-10-26):** he lunges when you're within **2 m** (was 1.6 m) and the hit lands if you're still within **2.5 m** (was 2.1 m) when his claws come down, so he's harder to juke.
  - **Frankie, more dangerous (2026-10-26):** **sight depends on light.** In pitch black with the torch off he sees you only within **4 m**; by a candle or a red emergency lamp about 6 m; standing under working lamps **15 m**; with your torch on **25 m**. **He no longer gives up after one hit:** after each swing he takes a **3-second breather** (a step back, staring at you), then comes straight back at you, until you die, escape out of sight, hide unseen or drive him off with a flare.
  - **Playtest fixes (2026-10-27)** (first friend playtest: "the AI is too easy, he never hit me"):
    - **The swipe lunges:** when you're within 2 m he leaps about **1.5 m forward** and the claws land after **0.5 s** (was 0.8 s standing still; the animation is sped up to match). Once he starts he turns only slowly, and the claws only catch you **in front of him** (within 50 degrees). Walking or running straight away gets you hit; a **sideways dodge** early in the swipe makes him miss; dodging too late doesn't.
    - **A miss means no breather:** he comes straight back at you. The 3 s breather only follows a hit that landed.
    - **Sprint stamina:** sprinting lasts about **6 s**, then you're **winded** and can only jog at **3.0 m/s** (Frankie runs 3.8) until you've got back 40% (about 3 s of not sprinting; full again after 8 s). No bar on screen: the heavy breathing tells you, building as stamina drains.
    - **He jumps anywhere along the platform edges:** jump links every 4 m along every platform edge at both stations (was 3-4 narrow spots, so he often walked the long way round to the tracks), and he **leaps in a short arc** (about 0.8 s) instead of gliding.
    - "I should turn around..." no longer appears on the Canal St escalators (it only belongs near the street's invisible walls).
- **Flashlight:** the battery drains and spare batteries are scarce.
- **Saving:** only in **safe rooms** (e.g. a staff break room with a working lamp).
  - **Built (2026-10-24):** the staff room PC is the save point ("Use computer (save game)"), plus **one autosave** 4 s after the power comes back (its own slot, so it never overwrites the player's). **5 named slots.** Choosing a slot opens a name box pre-filled with "<area> - <time>" (e.g. "Staff Room - 14:32"); Enter keeps it or type your own (24 letters max). **Load game** is on the title and in the pause menu (from the pause menu it asks first). A save keeps: where you stand, what you carry, torch battery and on/off, hits taken, fuses in the box, power, picked-up items, open doors, and whether the Pale Man is awake and where.
- **No sanity mechanic.**

## Look

- **Surface brightness:** concrete and gravel tints stay around 0.5–0.6. Near-black surfaces made the flashlight look useless in the tunnels.

- **Cleaner PS1:** low-poly PSX models with crunchy pixel-sharp textures, at **full resolution**, with no vertex wobble and no pixelation filter.
- **Lighting:** pools of light with near-black darkness between them.
  - Stations: mostly cold, greenish fluorescent tubes.
  - Street: warm sodium-orange lamps.
  - Tunnels: red emergency lights.
- **Fog:** things fade into black over about **25 m** (raised from 15 m after playtesting), but bright lamps still glow through from further away.

## Sound
- **Sound pass (2026-10-22):** each area has its own background sound, crossfading as you walk (`AreaAmbience`): street = rain loop + quiet "Tube Wind" drone (the rain muffles down the stairs and dies out along the corridor); station = "Too Quiet"; tunnels = "Always Observing". Underground, a distant noise (rockfall, gravel, metal scrape, door creak) plays every 60-120 s from 15-30 m away (`DistantNoises`). A leaking pipe loops above the track puddle. Footsteps: wet gravel on the tracks, splashes in puddles, concrete elsewhere. Notes and posters rustle when you start reading them; fuses clink, the key rattles.

- **Ambience, tension and machine hums:** Helton Yan's Surreal Drones (53-second loops).
- **One-shot effects:** Darkworld Audio – Survival Effects (footsteps, doors, switches, water, breathing, gun handling).
- **Music:** "PSX Horror Music" by Svirls, used **rarely**: chases, big reveals, power coming back, and credits.
- **Shotgun blast:** silent until a real shotgun sound is found.
- **Character sounds (2026-10-26):** Frankie's **scream when he spots you** (start of every chase) is now a real monster scream: the strongest 3 s of one of two close screams, with a quick fade. His **far-away echoing scream** plays when he wakes deep in Hollis St's west tunnel, and at Canal St from far back down the arrival tunnel about **6 s before** the thud on the train roof. The **player breathes**: heavy breathing builds up as sprint stamina drains (since 2026-10-27; it was a separate 4 s timer) and dies away as it refills; in a **locker** a slow, held breath plays, louder the closer Frankie is (from 10 m in). His breaths, footsteps and joint cracks are still the reshaped Darkworld sounds.
- **Still missing:** a shotgun blast, footsteps on metal grates, and real breathing/footstep sounds for Frankie.

## The truth: fear made flesh (world-building, decided 2026-10-28; NOT to implement yet)

- **The giant spider reveal is dropped** (too on the nose).
- **The cause is occult / paranormal:** a **liminal entity** under Line 4 makes people's nightmares real. It is never a creature you fight; it lives in empty, in-between, wrong-feeling spaces, and that liminality is what makes it creepy.
- **The real monster is fear.** Each victim becomes **what they were most afraid of**:
  - a worker **afraid of spiders** is turned into a spider-thing, and goes on to turn others, who become **the Wrapped** (cocooned) or the **eyeball / spider creatures**;
  - a worker **afraid of death** becomes a **Ridgeback**, shambling undead for eternity.
- The missing workers from the posters (Dale Kowalski, Rosa Mendez, Theo Banks) are among the victims; which fear each had is still to be decided.
- **Creature line-up (assets, not bought yet):**
  - **Frankie is a placeholder**, most likely to be replaced by the **Ridgeback** (Codyanka; much creepier and more threatening). **Keep Frankie's AI and everything behind the model** (senses, moods, lunge, lockers, jumps, sounds hooks): only the body and animations change. The Wrapped and Clawman (Codyanka) are the alternatives.
- **How the entity shows itself:** only as **shadow figures** at the corner of your eye or the edge of the darkness (like Hollis St's old shadow figure: near-black, there for a moment, gone when you look or come close). It is never seen clearly. (The old shadow-figure code is kept for this.)
- **Structure:** **each section of the game focuses on one fear**, and the **last one is the player character's own fear**.
- **The player character (decided 2026-10-28):** a **Portuguese migrant worker**, estranged from his family and friends because of deep-seated mental struggles (ADHD, OCD, anger issues, depression, derealisation). He deeply regrets his own existence and how he handled things in the past with his **parents** and his **former girlfriend**. No kids: just regrets and a bitter life. That's why he takes the job: nothing left to lose, nothing worth losing, or so he thinks. He can still lose his mind.
  - **His fear: being forgotten.** The final section is built from it.
  - **Revealed by slow hints** scattered through every station, small personal things that only make sense at the end.
  - **Two endings** (see below).
  - **Name: J. Sousa.** Workmates, dispatch and badges use only his surname ("Sousa", "J. Sousa").
  - **Details are inspired, fictional:** the feelings are real, but names, places and events are made up.
- **One fear per section (decided 2026-10-28):**
  - **Station 1, Hollis St: death.** The **Ridgeback** (the worker afraid of death, shambling undead forever) hunts you from the start.
  - **Station 2, Canal St: spiders.** The spider-fear worker's nest: the Wrapped and eyeball creatures, webs thickening the deeper you go.
  - **Station 3, the sewers: drowning / water.** Rising water, flooded tunnels, things moving under the surface.
  - **Station 4: losing your mind.** Derealisation made real: the station repeats, rooms rearrange, you can't trust what you see. The bridge into his own fear.
  - **The end: being forgotten**, Sousa's own fear.
- **The two endings are decided by reaching out:** optional moments of connection along the way (answering the calls, finding and keeping pieces of an unsent letter, and the like). Enough of them and **his parents** remember him: he walks out at dawn. Too few and he's forgotten and becomes one of the shadow figures.
- **The whole game is an allegory (decided 2026-10-28):** the metro is **Sousa's deep psyche**. Going down the line is searching through it; each station is a buried fear or wound; restoring the power is **working through his traumas bit by bit, in the dark, by himself, alone, as he always was**. He's a maintenance worker repairing his own mind. The title carries it too: the last stop is where he either gets off or doesn't.
- **The ending is "get better" or "die":** in the hopeful ending he climbs out, remembered by his parents; in the bleak one he walks off into the dark and becomes a ghost, one of the shadow figures (suicide, shown through that walk into the dark).
  - **Necrothread: The Pulse of Rotting Flesh** (imaginais: a person fused with flesh who collapses into a bone-and-limb spider with one eye) is for **later**, as its own monster.
  - The rest of imaginais' monsters (especially the **eyeball spider creatures**) fit the theme.
